01 STUDENT A'S CONTRIBUTION

Life on land is our shared home.

United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 15 promotes the protection, restoration, and sustainable use of terrestrial ecosystems.

Forests clean the air, store carbon, retain water, and provide habitats for many plants, animals, fungi, and microorganisms. Meadows are equally valuable: their flowering plants provide food for pollinators such as bees and butterflies.

By 2030, the United Nations aims to conserve terrestrial and inland freshwater ecosystems, promote sustainable forest management, restore degraded land, combat desertification, halt biodiversity loss, and protect threatened species.

When nature loses its balance, we all lose.

Our local environment also faces challenges that affect the health of ecosystems.

A / 01
🌿

Waste and Invasive Plants

Illegal dumping sites, plastic, and other waste pollute forest floors and endanger wildlife. Invasive non-native plants, such as Japanese knotweed, are another problem: they spread rapidly and can displace native plants.

POLLUTION · NON-NATIVE SPECIES
B / 02
🐝

Loss of Habitats and Pollinators

When natural meadows, hedgerows, and old trees disappear, animals lose food, shelter, and places to reproduce. Fewer flowering plants mean less food for bees, butterflies, and other pollinators.

BIODIVERSITY · HABITATS
✳

What can we do? Take our waste home, plant native species, preserve flowering areas, and respect wildlife habitats.
